Our name

SautiLume (say sah-OO-tee loom) joins two words: sauti, the Kiswahili word for voice, and lume, meaning light. Together they mean “voice of light”: health and education that reach young people through their own voice, and light the way to the care and learning they deserve.

"Across Africa, access to quality health care and education is often determined by more than clinics and classrooms alone. Information gaps, disability, gender inequality, and systemic barriers continue to shape who is reached and who is left behind."
Why we built this

Millions of blind and visually impaired young people can't reach the health and education information they need, because it was never made for them.

Health information and learning materials are almost always designed for people who can read print or a screen. For blind and visually impaired young people in Kenya, that leaves a gap with real consequences: questions that should be private get asked out loud to a parent, teacher or stranger, and learning that should be shared gets missed.

🎙️

Built on lived community work

SautiLume grew out of nearly two decades of direct work with young people with visual and cognitive disabilities in Kenyan schools and communities, not a lab exercise.

🩺

Grounded in the health and education systems

Our founder has worked within Kenya's health system as a Health Economist and Digital Health Specialist for more than a decade, and our Director of Accessibility and Inclusive Education teaches blind and visually impaired learners every day. SautiLume is designed to fit the referral pathways and classrooms they know firsthand, not replace them.

Health and education, together

The clinic and the classroom put up the same barrier. SautiLume answers both with one voice.

A young person who is blind or visually impaired meets the same wall at the clinic and at school: information made for people who can see print. Health and learning are also bound together. Young people who understand their bodies, feel safe and get support for their mental health are better placed to learn, and health knowledge is itself part of what Kenya's curriculum sets out to teach. SautiLume brings health and education into one private, voice-first companion.

Private, verified health answers spoken back in English or Kiswahili, with real referrals to health facilities, mental health support and helplines, and nothing stored.

Education

How SautiLume brings health and education together

🔗

Health learning, linked to class

Health is part of Kenya's curriculum, taught inside subjects such as Integrated Science. SautiLume links its verified health answers to the topics learners meet in class, so a private question and a classroom lesson reinforce each other.

🎧

Learning support by voice

Explanations, revision and practice spoken aloud, step by step, giving hints before answers so that learners do the thinking themselves.

🔬

Accessible maths and science

Spoken maths, described diagrams and audio support for science practicals and STEM projects, designed with teachers who already adapt lessons for blind and visually impaired learners.

🧑‍🏫

Support for teachers

Special needs teachers are in short supply and accessible books are scarce. SautiLume helps teachers prepare accessible audio and text faster, and gives them trusted answers to the health questions learners bring to school.

💙

Wellbeing that keeps learners learning

Support for stress, exam pressure, low mood and staying safe, with a clear path to a school counsellor, a trusted adult or a helpline, because learners who feel safe can focus on learning.

🧭

One pathway from school to care

Verified referrals that connect learners to health services, counselling and support, working alongside a school's guidance and counselling department and school health programme, not around them.

Built to fit Kenya's own frameworks

SautiLume is designed to support the policies that schools and health services already work to, not to sit outside them.

  • Kenya School Health Policy (2018). Developed jointly by the Ministries of Education and Health, it covers values and life skills, and the needs of learners with special needs and disabilities.
  • Sector Policy for Learners and Trainees with Disabilities (2018). Commits to specialised learning resources, assistive devices and new technologies for learners with disabilities.
  • Persons with Disabilities Act (2025). Protects the right to education, and recognises audio, software, Braille and other accessible formats in its definitions of assistive devices and communication.
  • WHO and UNESCO Global Standards for Health Promoting Schools (2021). Kenya was one of the first countries to pilot these standards, which make health part of every school's curriculum, environment and services.
  • UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities. Guarantees the right to inclusive education (Article 24) and to health care on an equal basis with others (Article 25).

Our learning features are being built step by step, with teachers, learners, schools and families. Before any activity with learners, we obtain written permission from the school and consent from parents or guardians, and learners are asked in a format they can access. Leading this work is Esther Bikeri, our Director of Accessibility and Inclusive Education.

How it works

Ask a question out loud. Get a verified answer back.

SautiLume is designed to be used entirely by voice, because our users can't rely on a screen. Health answers come from a knowledge base verified by health professionals, and learning content is co-designed with special needs educators, before anything is spoken back.

1

Speak your question

No login, no typing, no username. A young person simply asks about their health, a worry, their safety, or something they are learning at school.

2

SautiLume answers, out loud

The verified answer is spoken back right away, in the language the user is already speaking.

3

Real referrals when it matters

SautiLume never invents a phone number or a facility. It gives verified referrals only, covering public and private health facilities, mental health support, counselling, and pharmacies, right across the country.

4

Then it forgets

The conversation flows naturally while it's happening. The moment it ends, nothing is left behind on the device.

Privacy by design

SautiLume cannot leak, sell, or be forced to hand over what it never keeps.

For a young person asking a private question about their health, their safety, or their life at home or school, a stored conversation isn't just a data risk. It can be physical danger the moment someone else picks up the phone. So SautiLume keeps no names, no audio, no transcripts, and no summaries.

Safety by design

Nothing is stored, so there is nothing for a parent, teacher, or abuser to find on the phone afterward.

Legal strength

Zero retention is how we put the data-minimisation principle of Kenya's Data Protection Act 2019 into practice, including its stronger protections for children's data. In schools, we also work within the school's own consent process.

Trust the big platforms can't offer

Most technology is built to remember. SautiLume is built to forget, on purpose, every time.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

A quick, honest look at what SautiLume is, what it isn't, and how it handles your privacy.

Does SautiLume cover education as well as health?

Yes. SautiLume is a health and education platform. Health is where we started, with verified answers by voice and real referrals. The demo and app now also answer first questions about learning: revising for exams, books in Braille and audio, exam arrangements, screen readers, maths and science without sight, school fees and the right to education, and careers. With special needs teachers we are building much more: spoken explanations and revision, accessible maths and science, health topics linked to what learners meet in class, and tools that help teachers prepare accessible materials. These learning features are introduced step by step and tested with learners, with the consent of schools and of parents or guardians.

Is SautiLume solely dependent on AI?

No. SautiLume's core answers come from a verified knowledge base: health answers are reviewed by health professionals, and learning content is co-designed with special needs educators. It works even without AI. An optional AI layer can add more natural, conversational responses, but SautiLume is never solely dependent on it. If AI is unavailable, the verified library always has the final say.

Is my data stored when I use SautiLume?

No. SautiLume keeps no names, no audio, no transcripts, and no summaries. Once a conversation ends, nothing is left behind on the device.

How does SautiLume work with schools?

We work alongside teachers, guidance and counselling departments and school health programmes, never around them. Before any activity with learners, we obtain written permission from the school and consent from parents or guardians, and learners are asked in a format they can access, such as Braille, large print or audio. We do not record learners or keep notes that identify them.

Can I use SautiLume on an iPhone or an Android phone?

Yes. The voice demo works in Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Safari and Firefox, on Android phones, iPhones, iPads and computers. In Chrome, Edge and Safari you can speak your question. In Firefox, and in any browser that cannot listen, you can type it or use the microphone button on your phone's keyboard. To keep SautiLume on your phone like an app: on an iPhone, open the demo in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen; on Android, open the demo in Chrome, tap the menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app. Our Android app is in testing with pilot schools, and an iPhone app is planned.

What languages does SautiLume support?

SautiLume currently works in English and Kiswahili, with offline voice recognition for both languages in development.

Is SautiLume free of charge?

Yes. SautiLume is free of charge for all users. Our plan is to sustain the platform through institutional licensing with health, education, and disability-focused partners.

Where is SautiLume being tested?

SautiLume is being tested continuously, even at this early prototype stage, with visually impaired young people in Kitui, Kiambu, and Nairobi counties.

Does SautiLume replace a doctor, a health facility or a teacher?

No. SautiLume gives verified information and, when needed, verified referrals to real public and private health facilities, mental health support, counselling, and pharmacies. Its learning features support teachers and the curriculum; they do not replace them. It never invents a phone number, a diagnosis, or a fact.
