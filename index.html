Why we built this

Millions of young people can't reach the health information they need — because it was never made for them.

Reproductive health material, menstrual hygiene guidance, mental health support and consent education are almost always designed for people who can read text on a screen. For blind, visually impaired, and intellectually disabled young people in Kenya, that leaves a gap with real consequences — and often means asking a parent, teacher, or stranger a question that should be private.

🎙️

Built on lived community work

SautiLume grew out of nearly two decades of direct work with young people with visual and cognitive disabilities in Kenyan schools and communities — not a lab exercise.

🩺

Grounded in the health system

Our founder works inside Kenya's Ministry of Health as a Digital Health Specialist, so SautiLume is designed to fit real referral pathways, not replace them.

💙

Free for the young people who need it

SautiLume is free to users. The platform is sustained through institutional licensing with health, education, and disability-focused partners.

How it works

Ask a question out loud. Get a verified answer back.

SautiLume is designed to be used entirely by voice, because our users can't rely on a screen. Every step is built around that.

1

Speak your question

No login, no typing, no username. A young person simply asks — about a period, a symptom, a fear, or what "consent" means.

2

SautiLume answers, out loud

The answer is checked against a verified health knowledge base before it's spoken back, in the language the user is already speaking.

3

Real referrals when it matters

For safety or crisis questions, SautiLume never invents a phone number or a facility. It only ever gives verified contacts, like Befrienders Kenya.

4

Then it forgets

The conversation flows naturally while it's happening. The moment it ends, nothing is left behind on the device.

Privacy by design

SautiLume cannot leak, sell, or be forced to hand over what it never keeps.

For a young person asking about abuse or contraception, a stored conversation isn't just a data risk — it can be physical danger the moment someone else picks up the phone. So SautiLume keeps no names, no audio, no transcripts, and no summaries.

Safety by design

Nothing is stored, so there is nothing for a parent, teacher, or abuser to find on the phone afterward.

Legal strength

Zero retention is our compliance posture under Kenya's Data Protection Act 2019, not just a design choice.

Trust the big platforms can't offer

Most technology is built to remember. SautiLume is built to forget — on purpose, every time.

Where we are

Early stage, deliberately careful, building brick by brick.

SautiLume is in active development. We're building slowly and verifying every step, because our users are vulnerable and the answers have to be right.

  • Now

    Working voice pipeline. A voice-first Android prototype answers health questions end to end, with verified content and zero data retention.

  • Next

    Verified referral network. Building a database of real, verified health facilities and hotlines, starting with Kitui County, so referrals are never guessed.

  • Next

    Offline Swahili voice recognition. Moving to an offline speech engine so the app works reliably regardless of phone model or connectivity.

  • Pilot

    Kitui County, with the Kenya Institute of Special Education (KISE). Testing directly with the young people SautiLume is built for.

  • Seeking

    Partners who share this mission. We're in early conversations and welcome connections with organizations working in disability inclusion, adolescent health, and digital health in East Africa.
